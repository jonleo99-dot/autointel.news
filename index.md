    <p>Volkswagen Group is moving to sharply cut the number of unique headlights, bumpers, seats, and wheel designs used across its brand portfolio, from mainstream VW and Skoda models up through premium Audi and Porsche nameplates. The push comes as Chinese rivals erode the group's once-dependable China profits, forcing a hunt for savings everywhere engineering and procurement touch a vehicle. Fewer part numbers means faster development cycles and lower unit costs at a moment when margins are tightening across the board. But standardizing components across brands that have spent decades selling on distinct design identity is a delicate maneuver: buyers paying a premium for Audi or Porsche expect their cars to look and feel meaningfully different from a Skoda, not assembled from the same bin of parts.</p>
