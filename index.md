    <h2>Toyota, Nissan Halt Japan Production After 7.1 Earthquake Rattles Supply Chain</h2>
    <p>A 7.1-magnitude earthquake struck the Kumamoto region of southwestern Japan on July 28, killing at least 13 people and damaging roads, buildings, and power infrastructure. Though Toyota&#x27;s and Nissan&#x27;s plants some 93 miles from the epicenter escaped direct damage, both automakers halted production over supplier and safety risk. Toyota suspended all three of its area plants through Friday — hitting engine, hybrid, and Lexus assembly lines that export roughly 90 percent of Miyata-built Lexus ES, RX, UX, and NX volume to the U.S. — for an estimated 5,200 lost units. Nissan&#x27;s shutdown is partial and open-ended, affecting Infiniti QX80 and Nissan Armada output, with at least two regional parts suppliers also offline.</p>
    <div class='hero-tension'><b>The Tension:</b> Two of the industry&#x27;s most redundancy-obsessed manufacturers still couldn&#x27;t insulate output from a single regional supplier network — a reminder that just-in-time math looks great until the ground actually moves.</div>
